Why should you care about the PACIO Project?

The PACIO (Post-Acute Care InterOperability) Project is all about care coordination – Forty-five percent of Medicare patients who leave hospitals go into some kind of post-acute care. Post-acute care accounts for $74 billion in Medicare payments annually, so it is a large part of our healthcare system. Post-acute care patients are typically very complex and have multiple transitions in their healthcare journey.

When a person transitions between healthcare settings, including ambulatory care, acute care, long-term post-acute care (LTPAC), and home- and community-based services (HCBS) – their care is often fragmented and can lead to poor health outcomes, increased burden, and increased costs. Interoperable health information exchange has the potential to improve patient and provider communications and supports access to longitudinal health information that enables improved efficiencies, improved quality of care, and improved health outcomes. Data should be usable across the continuum of care, and beyond the traditional healthcare system – into the community.

About

Mission

The PACIO Project is a collaborative effort to advance interoperable health data exchange between post-acute care (PAC) and other providers, patients, and key stakeholders across health care and to promote health data exchange in collaboration with policy makers, standards organizations, and industry through a consensus-based, use case-driven approach.


PAC on FHIR

The primary goal of the PACIO Project is to establish a framework for the development of Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resource (FHIR) technical implementation guides and reference implementations that will facilitate health data exchange through standards-based use case-driven application programming interfaces (APIs).

Announcements

November 9, 2026

The PACIO Project Community has made available a poster, white paper, and demonstration materials describing the Transition of Care AI-Assisted (ToC-AI) Proof of Concept leveraging the Clinical Health AI-augmented Records Toolkit (CHART), developed for the 2026 American Medical Informatics Association (AMIA) Annual Symposium.

The proof of concept explores how AI-assisted clinical information retrieval, reconciliation, and transformation can complement HL7® FHIR® standards to support transitions of care. The approach emphasizes clinician review and source traceability.

October 2026

The PACIO Project Community introduced the PACIO Explorer Reference Client, a free, browser-based tool that enables health IT vendors, clinicians, and implementers to explore and test PACIO interoperability workflows.

The live PACIO Explorer application supports creating Transitions of Care (ToC) documents, minimally structured portable medical orders, and exploring PACIO Implementation Guide capabilities using compatible HL7® FHIR® servers.

PACIO invites implementers to explore the application and provide feedback to help guide its continued development.

October 18, 2026

The PACIO Project Community contributed to the poster Advancing Medication Safety Through Interoperability: PACIO Implementation Guides for Standardized Drug Therapy Management at Transitions of Care at the 2026 American College of Clinical Pharmacy (ACCP) Annual Meeting.

The poster highlights how the PACIO Standardized Medication Profile (SMP) and Transitions of Care (ToC) Implementation Guides support standardized medication information exchange, medication reconciliation, and coordination across care settings. It also describes interoperability testing conducted through national Connectathons.

October 17, 2026

Members of the PACIO Community developed the use case PACIO-Aligned Standardized Medication Profile Use Case: Child with Special Healthcare Needs, featured at the 2026 American College of Clinical Pharmacy (ACCP) Annual Meeting, examining how a PACIO-aligned Standardized Medication Profile (SMP) could support medication information exchange for children and youth with special healthcare needs.

The use case identifies medication safety challenges involving multiple dispensing locations, compounded medications, specialized nutrition requirements, and incomplete clinical information during transitions of care.
