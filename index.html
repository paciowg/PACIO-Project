Why should you care about the PACIO Project?

The PACIO (Post-Acute Care InterOperability) Project is all about care coordination – Forty-five percent of Medicare patients who leave hospitals go into some kind of post-acute care. Post-acute care accounts for $74 billion in Medicare payments annually, so it is a large part of our healthcare system. Post-acute care patients are typically very complex and have multiple transitions in their healthcare journey.

When a person transitions between healthcare settings, including ambulatory care, acute care, long-term post-acute care (LTPAC), and home- and community-based services (HCBS) – their care is often fragmented and can lead to poor health outcomes, increased burden, and increased costs. Interoperable health information exchange has the potential to improve patient and provider communications and supports access to longitudinal health information that enables improved efficiencies, improved quality of care, and improved health outcomes. Data should be usable across the continuum of care, and beyond the traditional healthcare system – into the community.

About

Mission

The PACIO Project is a collaborative effort to advance interoperable health data exchange between post-acute care (PAC) and other providers, patients, and key stakeholders across health care and to promote health data exchange in collaboration with policy makers, standards organizations, and industry through a consensus-based, use case-driven approach.


PAC on FHIR

The primary goal of the PACIO Project is to establish a framework for the development of Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resource (FHIR) technical implementation guides and reference implementations that will facilitate health data exchange through standards-based use case-driven application programming interfaces (APIs).

Announcements

February 10, 2025
The PACIO Standardized Medication Profile (SMP) implementation guide was approved for publication as STU-1 (Standard for Trial Use 1).

January 15, 2025
The PACIO Project successfully tests and demonstrates 12 PACIO and other FHIR Implementation Guides working together as a system of systems in the PACIO Integration Track, showing the art of what is possible in a realistic, complex healthcare scenario. The scenario included 12 different system roles and 100 system transactions, incorporating post-acute care assessments, advance directives, medication lists, medication reconciliation, cancer, prior authorization, directory services, care plan management, and care team management. A video of the demonstration is available.

September 22, 2024
The PACIO Project successfully tests and demonstrates how the Standardized Medication Profile (SMP) IG and the Transitions of Care Implementation Guide can be used to exchange a patient's transitions of care information necessary by many post-acute care disciplines. Medication reconciliation was also demonstrated using the data exchanged using the SMP IG. A video of the demonstration is available.

September 22, 2024
The PACIO Project successfully tests and demonstrates how the Advance Directives (ADI) Implementation Guide can be used to exchange structured Personal Advance Care Plans (PACPs), structured Portable Medical Orders (PMOs) (new), and unstructured Mental Health Advance Directives (MHAD).

July 18, 2024
The PACIO Project successfully tests and demonstrates how the Standardized Medication Profile (SMP) IG and the Transitions of Care Implementation Guide can be used to exchange a patient's transitions of care information necessary by many post-acute care disciplines. Medication reconciliation was also demonstrated using the data exchanged using the SMP IG using a more complex use case and integrating more patient medical record data. A video of the demonstration is available.

July 18, 2024
The PACIO Project successfully tests and demonstrates how the Advance Directives (ADI) Implementation Guide can be used to exchange structured Personal Advance Care Plans (PACPs), unstructured Portable Medical Orders (PMOs), and unstructured Mental Health Advance Directives (MHAD). The participants also tested a better way to revoke an advance directive.
